The science of letters and numbers

Ilm-e-Jafar is a classical Islamic tradition that works with the numerical values of Arabic letters — the Abjad system. Every letter carries a fixed number (Alif = 1, Baa = 2, Jeem = 3, and so on). By adding the letters of a person's name, their mother's name and a chosen purpose, practitioners derive a total that becomes the basis of a Loh (a numbered grid, like a magic square) and of Takseer — rows made by weaving the letters from the start and the end of the line, alternately.

Loh and Takseer

A Loh arranges the numbers derived from the total into a grid — 3×3 is the classic, and larger grids (5×5, 7×7, 9×9) follow the traditional Siamese method so every row, column and diagonal agrees. Takseer rows show the letter-weaving step by step until the line returns to its starting form — the completed cycle.

Quranic Istikhara

In the Mustahsila-e-Qurani method, the total of your name and question is worked through a published sequence to arrive at a Surah and an Ayat — a pointer for reflection alongside the regular Istikhara prayer. Scholars of the tradition always pair it with prayer, counsel and real effort.
